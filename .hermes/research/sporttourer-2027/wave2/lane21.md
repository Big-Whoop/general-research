### F17 — Steering-damper recall NHTSA 25V194000 (2024–2025 M 1000 XR + M 1000 R) — platform carry-over risk
- Claim: BMW recalled certain 2024–2025 M 1000 XR and M 1000 R (51 US units) over a defective steering damper with a loose valve pin that can cause an unexpected increase in steering effort and loss of control; the M 1000 RS, sharing the M chassis/steering architecture, is a plausible carry-over risk to watch.
- Source: https://www.rideapart.com/news/755684/bmw-recall-m1000xr-m1000r-steering-damper/ (RideApart, NHTSA-sourced)
- Verbatim: "BMW of North America, LLC (BMW) is recalling certain 2024-2025 M 1000 XR, and M 1000 R motorcycles. Some of these models might have a defective steering damper that has a loose valve pin within the housing, which can cause an unexpected increase in steering effort. The unexpected increase in steering effort can result in a loss of control, increasing the risk of a crash, injury, and even death. There are 51 potentially affected units and dealers will replace the steering damper, free of charge. … The safety recall was issued on March 27, 2025, and the NHTSA campaign number is 25V194000."
- Tier: **Verified** (NHTSA-sourced recall, read in full). Carry-over relevance to the RS is **Plausible** (shared platform, not yet demonstrated on the RS).

### F18 — Ignition-lock recall NHTSA 26V478000 (2020–2026 S 1000 RR, 16,736 US units) — platform carry-over risk
- Claim: BMW recalled 2020–2026 S 1000 RR (and a 2024 S 1000 RR US) motorcycles (16,736 US units) because vibration can fail an internal ignition-lock component, causing the instrument display, rear lighting, or ABS to fail, or an engine stall while riding; the M 1000 RS shares this electronics/ignition platform.
- Source: https://mousavi.law/recalls/26v478000 (NHTSA-sourced) + https://www.motorcycle.com/bikes/news/2020-2026-bmw-s-1000-rr-recalled-for-ignition-lock-failure-44668897 (Motorcycle.com) + https://www.rideapart.com/news/803269/bmw-s-1000-rr-ignition-recall/ (RideApart)
- Verbatim (mousavi.law): "BMW of North America, LLC (BMW) is recalling certain 2024 S 1000 RR US, and 2020-2026 S 1000 RR motorcycles. The vibration of the motorcycle may cause an internal component of the ignition lock to fail, which can cause the instrument display, rear lighting, and other systems such as ABS to fail, or cause an engine stall while riding." — "NHTSA campaign number 26V478000 … Component ELECTRICAL SYSTEM:IGNITION … Vehicles affected S 1000 RR … Model years 2021–2025 … 16,736 … Reported to NHTSA July 23, 2026." — (RideApart): "BMW Is Recalling Nearly 17,000 Of Its S 1000 RRs In The US Over Ignition Lock Issue."

### F19 — The $15,000 M 1000 RR engine warranty dispute (Motomillion) — documented, but with a critical nuance
- Claim: The widely-cited "$15,000 M 1000 RR engine warranty dispute" is the Motomillion case: the M 1000 RR suffered a "massive engine failure" (a loose cylinder-2 connecting-rod cap, i.e. faulty engine assembly, with no oil starvation) and BMW denied the warranty claim — but the denial was grounded in the owner's aftermarket SC-Project exhaust + ECU tune and a break-in rev limiter removed by a non-authorized technician (plus a 250 rpm over-rev), so it is NOT a clean "BMW engine defect" signal.
- Source: https://www.rideapart.com/news/714745/motomillion-bmw-m1000rr-engine-failure/ (RideApart, read in full) + https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JFB4hQ1HmXQ (Motomillion video)
- Verbatim: "it was discovered that the connecting rod cap of cylinder 2 was loose, but the bearing wasn't spun … it was just a loose rod cap with no signs of oil starvation—and on a very new and fresh engine at that. As such, Manny and the folks at Sill's Motor Sales determined the issue to be faulty engine assembly. Nevertheless, BMW refused to grant them a warranty claim on a new engine, citing findings like a slight 250 rpm over-rev and the premature removal of the bike's 600-mile break-in rev limiter." and "Firstly, the break-in limiter wasn't removed by an authorized BMW technician. Secondly, the bike was fitted with an aftermarket exhaust system and ECU tune." and "spending $15,000 USD on a new engine."
- Tier: **Verified** (documented, multi-source case, read in full). **Nuance for the report:** present as "a documented M 1000 RR engine-assembly fault (loose rod cap) where BMW denied warranty on modification grounds" — do NOT present as proof of a systemic M 1000 RR/RS engine defect.
