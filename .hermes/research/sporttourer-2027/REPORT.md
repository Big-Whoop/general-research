## 0. TL;DR — best result first

**The single best all-round MY2027 sport-tourer in Switzerland is the BMW R 1300 RS** (ab CHF 16'700–16'800, already labeled "2027"). It is the consensus benchmark of the category: the 1000PS CH test calls it "ein tolles Motorrad mit kaum Schwächen" and "solch ein seltener, klassischer Sporttourer"[1], and the author of the 5-bike 1000PS DE comparison test — who calls the *outgoing* R 1250 RS "der beste Sporttourer am Markt" — says the R 1300 RS "wird in diesen Tagen präsentiert … wenn die nochmal besser wird dann liegt die Latte richtig hoch"[2]. It is a genuine classic sport-tourer (not a crossover), current for MY2027 with a published CH price, and it balances sport and touring better than any rival in the set.

**The strongest value + reliability alternative is the Suzuki GSX-S1000GT** (CHF 16'495): MCN rates it 5/5 — "a hell of a sports tourer for the money"[3], owners rate reliability 4.2/5 with "no issues" reports, and it is the only core-set bike with **no MY2022–2027 recalls** on the official Suzuki list (only a 2016 front-brake-caliper campaign).

**Two structural findings shape the whole picture:**
1. **MY2027 re-pricing is largely unpublished in CH.** As of 2026-10-17, **only BMW** has advanced a core-set model to a "2027" price (R 1300 RS ab 16'700; plus the new R 1300 RT ab 21'900). Every other core-set bike (S 1000 XR, M 1000 XR, F 900 XR, Ninja 1100SX, Tracer 7, Multistrada V4, Tiger Sport 800, GSX-S1000GT) still carries its MY2025/26 price — so any MY2027 price for them is *erwartet/unbestätigt* (Speculative) until the importers advance their model-year labels (wave2/lane25.md §E).
2. **The BMW M 1000 RS is the headline MY2027 new model but is not yet officially announced.** It is EPA/CARB-certified for 2027 (a sport-touring M 1000 RR, ~205 hp US / ~218 PS EU expected, EIM 280 kg)[5], but there is **no CH price, no CH spec sheet, no pro test, and no owner data** yet (wave2/lane21.md). Treat it as "to watch," not a buyable option, until the EICMA reveal.

**The three genuinely new MY2026/27 sport-tourers to feature:** BMW M 1000 RS (new MY2027, unannounced), Honda CB 1000 GT (new MY2026, on sale Feb 2026), Kawasaki Ninja 1100SX (new MY2026, replaces the Ninja 1000). Plus the KTM 1290 Super Duke GT is **end-of-line** — replaced by the all-new 1390 Super Duke GT for MY2027 (wave2/lane23.md).

## 1. Scope & method

**Question:** Which are the top sport-touring motorcycles for MY2027 (on sale in Switzerland in 2026/2027)?

**Six axes covered per bike:** (1) performance/specs, (2) professional tests + measured results, (3) CH prices (CHF incl. 8.1% VAT), (4) owner/user experiences, (5) reliability/defects/recalls/TSBs, (6) head-to-head comparisons + MY2026→MY2027 changes.

**Corpus:** 10 lane files under this directory (5 wave-1 single-axis lanes + 5 wave-2 gap-fill lanes). The wave-2 lanes closed the gaps wave-1 left: the BMW M 1000 RS (specs only), the Honda CB 1000 GT + Yamaha Tracer 9 (full deep-dive), the KTM 1290 Super Duke GT (the *actual* sport-tourer, not the naked R), the Suzuki GSX-S1000GT (tests/owners/reliability), and the head-to-head comparison tests + MY2027 re-pricing sweep.

**Candidate-set discipline:** the orchestrator's seed mixed true sport-tourers with naked bikes, superbikes, and discontinued models. The lanes corrected this (specs/lane1.md §TL;DR, tests/lane3.md §0). This report ranks the **true sport-tourers** first and keeps the naked/supersport entries clearly labeled as a different category.

## 4. Per-bike profiles (the six axes)

### 4.1 BMW R 1300 RS — the benchmark
- **Specs:** 1300 cc air/liquid boxer, 145 PS (107 kW) @ 7,750 rpm, 149 Nm @ 6,500 rpm; 245 kg fahrfertig; 17 L tank; 240 km/h top (BMW); 790 mm seat; first production telescopic fork with electronically adjustable spring rate (DSA). (specs/lane1.md F-05/F-06; tests/lane3.md F2.1–F2.3.)
- **Tests:** 1000PS CH (10.03.2026): "Die BMW R 1300 RS ist ein tolles Motorrad mit kaum Schwächen und vielen Stärken … solch ein seltener, klassischer Sporttourer"[1]. WMTC 4.8 l/100 km, real range >300 km. Wind protection "in Ordnung, der Helm steht recht im Wind."
- **Price:** ab CHF 16'700 (overview) / 16'800 (detail) — report as "ab 16'700–16'800"; already labeled "2027"[6].
- **Owners:** "Power ohne Ende," sportier than the R 1250 RS, comfortable; **gotchas** — 80,000 km driveshaft at owner's cost (~€970, BMW covers R 1200/1250 retroactively but *not* the R 1300), the Performance variant cannot mount panniers (slim rear), no centre stand, narrow mirrors. (owners/lane4.md F11–F17.)
- **Reliability:** R 1300 platform starter-relay recall (NHTSA 24V-557, fire risk, ~25,000 units worldwide) is a platform concern for the RS; early clutch-failure reports (community); clunky gearbox in the lower gears (mitigated by the ASA option). (reliability/lane5.md F6–F8.)
- **MY2027:** minor — left/right case holders now a factory option (Style Performance); no engine change. (specs/lane1.md F-07.)

### 4.2 BMW S 1000 XR / M 1000 XR — the sport/tour crossovers
- **Specs:** S 1000 XR 170 PS / 114 Nm, 227 kg; M 1000 XR 201 PS / 113 Nm, 223 kg, winglets (~12 kg downforce @ 220 km/h), 0–100 in 3.2 s, top >275 km/h. (specs/lane1.md F-01–F-04; tests/lane3.md F1.1–F1.2.)
- **Tests:** M 1000 XR (1000PS CH Intensiv-Test): "gelungene Demonstration der modernsten, sportlichsten Motorradtechnologie im tourentauglichen Kleid" — but not a real tourer (no cases, no adaptive cruise, high-speed vibrations, limited wind protection)[10]. WELT: "Wer lieber tourt ist mit der S besser bedient."
- **Price:** S 1000 XR ab 19'480 ("2026 | NEW"); M 1000 XR ab 25'980 ("2026"); no separate "2027" price block yet (M page image paths already reference a 2027 gallery). (prices/lane2.md F1–F2.)
- **Owners:** S 1000 XR reliable (15k–20k+ mile reports) but early-gen bar vibration + poor heated grips + weak halogen light + wind buffeting; a detailed Swiss (toeff-forum.ch) report: "gutes Motorrad … kaufen würd ich sie nicht." (owners/lane4.md F1–F10.)
- **Reliability:** M 1000 XR 2024–25 steering-damper recall (NHTSA 25V194000); S 1000 XR 2020–23 software (wheelie-control) recall (NHTSA 23V-051). (reliability/lane5.md F1–F2.)
- **MY2027:** S 1000 XR facelift (new central-headlight front, taller windscreen, *possible* radar, slimmer rear) is **homologation-based (Plausible)**, not an official BMW presentation; M 1000 XR gets new M Track Package / M Track Package II (M Competition Package dropped). (specs/lane1.md F-01/F-02.)

### 4.3 BMW F 900 XR — the lighter twin
- **Specs:** 895 cc parallel-twin, 105 PS / 92 Nm; 216 kg (3 kg lighter for MY2025); 215–217 km/h top. (specs/lane1.md F-08; tests/lane3.md F7.1.)
- **Tests:** motorradtest.de: "fährt sich sehr sportlich … extrem handlich … nahezu spielerisch"; "Der Motor wirkt deutlich kräftiger, als die 105 Pferde … vermuten lassen."
- **Price:** ab 12'650. (prices/lane2.md F4.)
- **Owners:** a 100,000 km owner calls it "a great reliable bike"; low running cost (~$400–700/yr); needs revving for best power. (owners/lane4.md F18–F21.)
- **Reliability:** shaft final-drive lurch, clunky gearbox, one clutch-cable break at 17,000 km (community); a "Loncin-made engine" forum claim is **Speculative/likely inaccurate** — do not carry as fact. (reliability/lane5.md F10–F14.)
- **MY2027:** Comfort Package gains the M Endurance Chain; no engine change. (specs/lane1.md F-09.)

### 4.4 BMW M 1000 RS — the new MY2027 flagship (unannounced)
- **Specs (expected):** sport-touring version of the M 1000 RR, same 999 cc inline-four; ~205 hp (US) / ~218 PS (EU) @ 13,000 rpm, 113 Nm @ 11,100 rpm; EIM 280 kg (~10 kg heavier than the RR). (specs/lane1.md F-10; wave2/lane21.md.)
- **Status:** EPA/CARB-certified for 2027 (authoritative that the model exists and is MY2027)[5]; **no official CH price, no CH spec sheet, no pro test, no owner data** yet. Official announcement expected fall 2026 (EICMA). (wave2/lane21.md.)
- **Reliability:** none documented yet (platform = M 1000 RR, which had a warranty-dispute case but no XR/RS-specific recall).
- **MY2027:** NEW. **Action:** re-probe after the EICMA reveal for the CH price + spec + first tests.

### 4.5 Kawasaki Ninja 1100SX SE — the new MY2026 four
- **Specs:** 1,099 cc inline-four, 136 PS @ 9,000 rpm, 113 Nm @ 7,600 rpm (Euro 5+); 235 kg curb; 19 L tank; 835 mm seat (EU high seat); SE adds Brembo M4.32 + Öhlins S46. (specs/lane1.md F-11/F-12; tests/lane3.md F6.1–F6.4; wave2/lane25.md.)
- **Tests:** 1000PS DE: "ein hervorragender Sporttourer für Fahrer, die Komfort und Dynamik gleichermaßen suchen." Negatives: relatively high consumption (5.7 official / 6–7.5 test), fork weak in response, **low payload (195 kg)**, operation not always intuitive. (tests/lane3.md F6.5.)
- **Price:** ab 17'300 (SE; kawasaki.ch lists only the SE in the CH Sport Tourer range; a base 1100SX appears at dealers ~15'590). (prices/lane2.md F12; wave2/lane25.md F5.3.)
- **Owners:** 1000SX (predecessor) sentiment used as proxy — trusted long-term reliability, but **expensive to maintain** (valve checks + hard-to-access engine + poor economy = "one of the most expensive motorcycles to own long-term"). (owners/lane4.md F22–F26.)
- **Reliability:** predecessor (Ninja 1000/Z1000SX) history — intermittent electrical, oil/water-pump gasket leaks (esp. 2016), sticky clutch (2011–13), stalling; a 120,000-mile no-valve-check durability report is the positive counterweight. Whether these carry to the 1100SX is unconfirmed. (reliability/lane5.md F15–F18.)
- **MY2027:** confirmed to return (US "GT+"-style naming; Europe-only for now); no substantial mechanical change. (wave2/lane25.md F6.)

### 4.6 Honda CB 1000 GT — the new MY2026 Fireblade-DNA tourer
- **Specs:** 1000 cc inline-four (Fireblade-derived, shared with the CB1000 Hornet), 110.1 kW (149.7 PS / 147.6 hp) @ 11,000 rpm, 102 Nm @ 8,750 rpm; 229 kg (241 kg with standard panniers); 21 L tank; 200 km/h limited; Showa EERA semi-active suspension, 6-axis IMU, cornering ABS, 5 ride modes, quickshifter, cruise, heated grips, centre stand, standard panniers. (specs/lane1.md F-15/F-16; wave2/lane22.md F2–F8.)
- **Tests:** too new (on sale in CH from Feb/March 2026) for a measured pro test this run; Bennetts cross-checks the official figures. (wave2/lane22.md F4–F8.)
- **Price:** CHF 13'690 (single CH trim). (prices/lane2.md — via wave2/lane22.md F1.)
- **Owners:** too new for long-term reports ("No user reports yet"); early sentiment positive (smooth power, touring comfort, value). (wave2/lane22.md F9–F12.)
- **Reliability — the key caveat:** the GT shares its exact engine with the **CB1000 Hornet, which is being recalled across the US/UK/Germany for excessive oil consumption** (NHTSA Dec 2025, 452 US units; UK all Hornets; 2,577 units in Germany; measured up to 400 ml/1,000 miles; the Fireblade SC59 ancestor had the same trait). Honda said the GT/F "will implement any required changes" (production Q2 2026) — but **whether the specific piston/ring fix was applied to the GT's MY2026 build is not independently confirmed.** Treat the GT's oil-consumption status as *Plausible-fixed / unconfirmed*. (wave2/lane22.md F13–F18; reliability/lane5.md F23–F26.)
- **MY2027:** NEW MY2026; MY2027 = carryover expected (Speculative).

### 4.7 Yamaha Tracer 9 — the tech triple (new MY2025)
- **Specs:** 890 cc CP3 triple, 87.5 kW (119 PS / 117 hp) @ 10,000 rpm, 93 Nm @ 7,000 rpm; 212 kg (base) / 227 kg (GT) / 232 kg (GT+ Y-AMT); 18.9 L tank; 845 mm seat; GT adds Matrix LED + KADS semi-active; GT+ adds Y-AMT + forward radar (ACC 30–160 km/h). (specs/lane1.md F-19/F-20; wave2/lane22.md F20–F26.)
- **Tests:** 1000PS DE (Tiger Sport 800 vs Tracer 9 GT+): the Tracer 9 GT+ "zeigt eindrucksvoll, wie sinnvoll Technik eingesetzt werden kann … ist sie eine Offenbarung"[4]. 58 mpg touring / 48 mpg manic (Bennetts). (wave2/lane22.md F23; wave2/lane25.md F2.3.)
- **Price:** 12'490 (base) / 15'690 (GT) / 18'990 (GT+ Y-AMT), incl. 8.1% VAT. (wave2/lane22.md F19.)
- **Owners:** strongly positive ("about perfect; sporty and fun while still being very reliable and comfortable"); a 10,000 km and a 35,000 km owner are both positive (the 35k owner's only issue was a TPS replaced under warranty). (wave2/lane22.md F27–F29.)
- **Reliability — the key caveat:** a **recurring electronic (TPS/ECU) issue**, not mechanical — unstable idling/stall (NHTSA 25V248000, 2021–2025 Tracer 9 GT; Malaysia JPJ 1,369 units; one owner documents a recall "every year" 2022–2025). Post-fix feedback is mixed ("runs better than new" vs "this fix may not work"). The MY2025 build should carry the latest ECU/TPS fix, but the specific CH VIN range is unconfirmed. (wave2/lane22.md F31–F36.)
- **MY2027:** NEW MY2025; carryover expected.

### 4.8 Yamaha Tracer 7 — the budget twin
- **Specs:** 689 cc CP2 twin, 73 PS / 68 Nm; 203 kg (Standard) / 212 kg (GT); 18 L tank; MY2025 update added throttle-by-wire + rider aids (power unchanged). (specs/lane1.md F-21; tests/lane3.md F3.1–F3.2.)
- **Tests:** 1000PS DE: "schlicht besser geworden" — agile, fun, comfortable; but limited wind protection, quickshifter only optional, Y-AMT from 2026. 73 PS is limited with full load + pillion (consider the Tracer 9 for that). (tests/lane3.md F3.4/F3.6.)
- **Price:** ab 9'490 (still the 2024 PDP; no MY2027 re-pricing). (prices/lane2.md F22; wave2/lane25.md F5.4.)
- **Owners:** standout comfort ("700 km/day without back pain"); two new Tracer 700s ran 55k and 78k km with only a broken clutch cable; minor rust + suspension niggles. (owners/lane4.md F32–F35.)
- **Reliability:** no widely-reported critical issues; clutch rattle (worn clutch-bolt dampers) is the common minor complaint; the Tracer 900 battery-drain issues are a *different* model (do not conflate). (reliability/lane5.md F27–F32.)
- **MY2027:** MY2025 update; carryover.

### 4.9 Ducati Multistrada V4 (family) — the V4 crossover
- **Specs:** 1,103 cc Desmosedici Stradale V4, 170–180 PS (varies by variant/Euro 5+ mapping) / 118 Nm; >230 kg dry; semi-active suspension (Öhlins Smart EC 2.0 on V4 S; Skyhook DSS on some); 6-axis IMU; STM-EVO dry clutch. (specs/lane1.md F-26.)
- **Tests:** crazywheels.it GPS: top speed 259.4 km/h, 0–100 ~3.2 s, 100–0 ~38.0 m; 1,500 km average ~6.25 l/100 km. Verdict: "a superbike disguised as a globetrotter" — wins on emotion/road chassis vs the R 1300 GS, loses on practicality/off-road (too heavy). (tests/lane3.md F4.1–F4.4; wave2/lane25.md F7.2.)
- **Price:** V4 ab 19'990 → V4 S 25'290 → V4 S Grand Tour 28'490 → V4 Rally 29'290 → V4 Pikes Peak 33'190 → V4 RS 39'990 (all incl. 8.1% VAT). (prices/lane2.md F5–F10; wave2/lane25.md F5.5.)
- **Owners:** 24k-mile owner no issues; a long-term Team-BHP owner is highly positive ("Swiss Army Knife"); but **poor fuel range (175–200 miles)**, strong engine braking, not cheap. (owners/lane4.md F38–F42.)
- **Reliability — the key caveat:** **recurring valve-guide recalls** (NHTSA 21V212000, 2021, 129 units; then again on the new V4 S within months) — the single most important reliability flag for the V4 line; confirm the MY2027 build/supplier batch is clear. (reliability/lane5.md F40–F42.)
- **MY2027:** adds a "V4 GT" variant; the V4 Pikes Peak gets a 100th-anniversary livery (CH price unchanged 33'190). (specs/lane1.md F-27; wave2/lane25.md F6.3.)

### 4.10 Triumph Tiger Sport 800 — the value triple
- **Specs:** 798 cc inline-three, 115 PS / 84 Nm; 214 kg; 835 mm seat; 18.5–18.6 L tank; standard quickshifter, 3 ride modes, fully adjustable Showa suspension. (specs/lane1.md F-25; tests/lane3.md F5.1.)
- **Tests:** 1000PS DE (vs Tracer 9 GT+): "So ein Preis-Leistungs-Verhältnis gibt es sonst bei keinem neuen Crossover-Motorrad"[4]; heise: "Der Motor macht süchtig … fährt sich total fluffig und ist nicht zu schwer"; consumption 4.7–4.9 l/100 km, range >380 km. (tests/lane3.md F5.2–F5.4; wave2/lane25.md F2.2.)
- **Price:** ab 12'095 (MY25; NEW MY26 models added — Tiger Sport 660, Tiger Sport 800 Tour — but the base price is unchanged). (prices/lane2.md F25; wave2/lane25.md F5.6.)
- **Owners:** split — "bulletproof" / "reliability was perfect" reports vs frequent engine oil leaks (Tiger 800 platform) and low-speed surging/heat-stall. (owners/lane4.md F37–F39; reliability/lane5.md F37–F39.)
- **Reliability:** the Speed Triple 1200 (sibling) had radiator-fan + engine-overheat + rear-rotor-bolt recalls (2022–2024); the Tiger 800 platform's recurring concern is oil leaks. (reliability/lane5.md F33–F39.)
- **MY2027:** carryover; the only core-set bike with a **direct head-to-head result** in the corpus (test 2).

### 4.11 Suzuki GSX-S1000GT — the value + reliability pick
- **Specs:** 999 cc inline-four (GSX-R1000-derived), 152 PS / 106 Nm; 226 kg official / 231 kg measured (1000PS scale); 19 L tank; 810 mm seat; cruise control + Suzuki mySpin; TRAVEL edition adds smart cruise control, cornering ABS, SRAS. (specs/lane1.md F-31; wave2/lane24.md F1.2–F1.9.)
- **Tests:** MCN 5/5 — "effortlessly quick, comfortable, practical, reassuring and, thanks to its K5-derived engine, exciting … a hell of a sports tourer for the money"[3]; 1000PS CH Reisecheck (2000 km/7 days over the Alps): "Eine Reiseüberraschung allererster Güte." Critiques: ABS "not quite up to date" (no 6-axis IMU), sluggish cruise control, rear oscillation in fast bumpy curves. (wave2/lane24.md F1.1, F1.6, F1.8–F1.9.)
- **Price:** 16'495 (base) / 17'995 (TRAVEL SE); price valid to 31.12.2026. (prices/lane2.md F16/F18; wave2/lane25.md F5.7.)
- **Owners:** MCN owners' reliability 4.2/5; "No issues and I still love it"; 8,000-mile owner "does it all extremely well"; recurring minor: cam-chain-tensioner tapping, stock seat uncomfortable for some, stock tires wear ~7,000 miles. (wave2/lane24.md F2.1–F2.11.)
- **Reliability:** the official Suzuki US recall list (refreshed Oct 15, 2026) shows **only** the 2016 GSX-S1000 front-brake-caliper recall for this platform — **no MY2022–2027 recalls**; two minor TSBs (engine calibration Aug 2023; brake-lamp-switch + cruise control Dec 2022). "No significant recurring engine faults have been widely reported." (wave2/lane24.md F3.1–F3.6.)
- **MY2027:** MY2025 update (new colors + Euro 5+); MY2026 current; **confirmed for MY2027** (US GT+ $14,599, no substantial mechanical changes). (wave2/lane24.md F4.1–F4.5.)

### 4.12 KTM 1290 Super Duke GT — end-of-line (→ 1390 GT MY2027)
- **Specs:** 1,301 cc 75° V-twin, 180 PS / 140 Nm; KTM's **true sport-tourer** (the faired GT, not the naked R). (wave2/lane23.md.)
- **Price:** CHF 22'790 (action: CHF 18'790). (wave2/lane23.md.)
- **Status:** **end-of-line** — MY2027 replaces it with the all-new **1390 Super Duke GT** (delayed to 2027 due to KTM's financial restructuring). The GT is **NOT** in the wiring-harness recall scope (that recall covers the MY2020–22 Super Duke R). (wave2/lane23.md.)
- **Action:** for a MY2027 purchase, the relevant KTM is the incoming **1390 Super Duke GT**, not the outgoing 1290 GT.

## 5. Head-to-head comparison tests

The three 1000PS DE comparison tests (read in full in wave2/lane25.md) are **2025 tests of MY2024/2025 bikes** and **do not directly benchmark the MY2027 core set** — they skew to crossover/tourer and cover the *outgoing* R 1250 RS (not the R 1300 RS), the Tracer 9 GT+ (890 cc CP3, not the Tracer 7), and the Versys 1100 (crossover, not the Ninja 1100SX). The **only core-set bike directly tested is the Triumph Tiger Sport 800** (test 2). Use them as cross-bike context, not as MY2027 core-set results.

- **Test 1 — "5 Reisemotorräder im Vergleich"** (R 1250 RT, R 1250 RS, GSX-S1000GX, R 1300 GS Adventure, Versys 1100): the author's favorite is the outgoing **R 1250 RS = "der beste Sporttourer am Markt"**, and he flags the R 1300 RS "wird in diesen Tagen präsentiert … wenn die nochmal besser wird dann liegt die Latte richtig hoch"[2]. The Versys 1100 is "the biggest surprise … sie fährt einfach gut" (but ugly).
- **Test 2 — Tiger Sport 800 vs Tracer 9 GT+ Y-AMT**: an "unequal comparison"; the engine is a matter of taste; **rider height decides** (Yamaha = better ergonomics + wind protection for tall riders, Triumph = easier for short-legged). The Tiger Sport 800 wins on **price/performance** ("So ein Preis-Leistungs-Verhältnis gibt es sonst bei keinem neuen Crossover-Motorrad"); the Tracer 9 GT+ wins on tech/wind protection ("a revelation").
- **Test 3 — NT1100 DCT ES / Versys 1100 SE / Tracer 9 GT+**: "three bikes, three personalities" — no single winner, matched to rider type (NT1100 = safety/tech-oriented; Versys 1100 SE = experienced sporty tourers, "auch für große Fahrer"; Tracer 9 GT+ = sporty tech-curious).

**Cross-cutting signals for the core set:** (1) the **R 1300 RS is the benchmark** the new models must beat; (2) **price/performance** is the recurring differentiator for the value picks (Tiger Sport 800, Versys 1100 SE); (3) **rider height/ergonomics** is a recurring deciding axis (Tiger Sport 800 low 835 mm seat = accessible; Tracer 7 / Multistrada V4 / S 1000 XR = taller/longer-reach). No other major *written* MY2027 head-to-head group test covering the full core set was found (a genuine gap). (wave2/lane25.md §D, §G.)

## 6. Reliability & recalls — the picture

Two different "reliability stories" dominate the new MY2026/27 entrants, and they are different in kind (wave2/lane22.md §3):

- **Honda CB 1000 GT = mechanical platform risk (oil consumption).** The Fireblade-derived engine is the same one being recalled across the US/UK/Germany for excessive oil consumption that can lead to engine damage. Honda committed to "required changes" for the GT (not yet on sale at recall time) but the specific fix is **not independently documented**. This is the heavier, more consequential consideration. (wave2/lane22.md F13–F18.)
- **Yamaha Tracer 9 = electronic platform risk (TPS/ECU).** A recurring TPS/ECU fault (stutter, cold-running, unstable idle/stall) addressed by repeated recalls/reflashes. Less consequential (no engine damage) but more frequent; the MY2025 build should carry the latest fix. (wave2/lane22.md F31–F36.)

**Per-bike reliability flags (most important first):**
- **Ducati Multistrada V4:** recurring valve-guide recalls (the key flag; confirm MY2027 batch clear).
- **BMW R 1300 RS:** 80,000 km driveshaft at owner's cost (~€970); R 1300 platform starter-relay recall (fire risk); early clutch reports.
- **Honda CB 1000 GT:** Hornet platform oil-consumption recall (GT fix unconfirmed).
- **Yamaha Tracer 9:** recurring CP3 TPS/ECU recalls (electronic).
- **BMW S/M 1000 XR:** 2024–25 steering-damper (M) + 2020–23 software (S) recalls (both predate MY2027).
- **Triumph (Tiger 800 platform):** recurring engine oil leaks; Speed Triple 1200 (sibling) fan/overheat/rotor-bolt recalls.
- **Kawasaki Ninja 1100SX:** predecessor (1000SX) electrical/gasket/clutch history (carry-over unconfirmed).
- **Suzuki GSX-S1000GT:** the cleanest — no MY2022–27 recalls, 4.2/5 owner reliability.
- **KTM 1290 Super Duke GT:** NOT in the wiring-harness recall scope (that's the naked R).

**Brand-level context (directional only, not MY2027 model-level):** Japanese brands (Yamaha, Suzuki, Honda, Kawasaki) consistently out-reliability the European makers in surveys; BMW ranks lower on raw reliability but has a high repurchase rate (~68%). (reliability/lane5.md F51–F53.)

## 8. Recommendation (ranked, by use case)

1. **Best all-round classic sport-tourer (the benchmark) — BMW R 1300 RS.** The only bike that is simultaneously a genuine classic sport-tourer, the pro-press benchmark ("kaum Schwächen"), the comparison-test authors' reference point, and current for MY2027 with a published CH price (16'700–16'800). Best balance of sport + tour + tech. *Caveats:* 80,000 km driveshaft at owner's cost (~€970), Performance variant can't mount panniers, no centre stand, R 1300 platform starter-relay recall + early clutch reports.
2. **Best value + reliability (the sensible pick) — Suzuki GSX-S1000GT.** MCN 5/5 "a hell of a sports tourer for the money," 4.2/5 owner reliability, and the only core-set bike with **no MY2022–27 recalls**. If you weight reliability + value over "benchmark" status, this is the strongest alternative. *Caveats:* no 6-axis IMU (older ABS), sluggish cruise control, no centre stand.
3. **Best sport/tour crossover (fastest, most tech, sporty rider) — BMW M 1000 XR / S 1000 XR.** 201/170 PS, winglets, 0–100 in 3.2 s; the M is superb as a sport machine but weak as a pure tourer (no cases, high-speed vibration, limited wind protection) — "wer lieber tourt ist mit der S besser bedient." *Caveats:* the S 1000 XR MY2027 facelift is still homologation-based (Plausible).
4. **Best budget + accessibility (shorter riders, first big bike) — Triumph Tiger Sport 800.** Best price/performance of any new crossover (ab 12'095), low 835 mm seat, "total fluffig." *Caveats:* Tiger 800 platform oil leaks; weak wind protection for tall riders.
5. **Best tech + triple character — Yamaha Tracer 9.** CP3 triple, Y-AMT, radar ACC, Matrix LED (ab 12'490 → 18'990 GT+). *Caveats:* recurring electronic (TPS/ECU) recall history; the MY2025 CH fix status is unconfirmed.
6. **Best value Fireblade-DNA — Honda CB 1000 GT.** 149.7 PS in a 229 kg Fireblade-derived package at 13'690, standard panniers + centre stand. *Caveats:* the Hornet platform oil-consumption recall; the GT's specific fix is unconfirmed; too new for long-term owner data.
7. **Best new-for-MY2027 to watch — BMW M 1000 RS.** The flagship sport-touring M 1000 RR (~218 PS EU expected), but **not yet officially announced** — no CH price/spec/tests/owners. Buy only after the EICMA reveal + first tests.
8. **End-of-line (buy the successor) — KTM 1290 Super Duke GT.** Replaced by the all-new 1390 Super Duke GT for MY2027; for a MY2027 purchase the relevant KTM is the 1390 GT, not the outgoing 1290 GT.

**If forced to name one:** the **BMW R 1300 RS** — it is the consensus benchmark, a genuine classic sport-tourer, and the only top pick that is fully current for MY2027 with a known CH price. The **Suzuki GSX-S1000GT** is the best choice if reliability + value outrank the benchmark's drivetrain gotchas.

## 9. What remains untested / unconfirmed on the exact target

Be explicit about what is **not** yet verified for MY2027 in CH:

- **BMW M 1000 RS — entirely unannounced.** No CH price, no CH spec sheet, no pro test, no owner data. Only EPA/CARB certification confirms it exists and is MY2027[5]. The EU/CH power (~218 PS) is inferred from the M 1000 RR, not officially published.
- **MY2027 re-pricing — unpublished for 9 of 10 core-set bikes.** Only BMW has advanced a "2027" CH price. Any MY2027 price for S/M 1000 XR, F 900 XR, Ninja 1100SX, Tracer 7, Multistrada V4, Tiger Sport 800, GSX-S1000GT is *erwartet/unbestätigt* (Speculative) until the importers advance their labels.
- **S 1000 XR MY2027 facelift — homologation-based (Plausible).** The new front/radar/slimmer rear is from Australian re-homologation data, not an official BMW presentation; radar is "possible," not confirmed.
- **Honda CB 1000 GT — oil-consumption fix unconfirmed.** Whether the GT's MY2026 build received the Hornet piston/ring fix is not independently documented (Honda's "required changes" is a commitment, not a published spec delta).
- **Yamaha Tracer 9 — MY2025 CH ECU/TPS fix unconfirmed.** The recall build windows (e.g. Malaysia Nov 2021–Feb 2025) may not exactly match the CH MY2025 VIN range.
- **No single head-to-head comparison test covers the full MY2027 core set.** The 1000PS DE tests are 2025 tests of MY2024/25 bikes; only the Tiger Sport 800 is a core-set bike directly tested.
- **No MY2027-specific measured performance delta tests** for the carryover bikes (Tracer 7, Tiger Sport 800, Ninja 1100SX, F 900 XR, R 1300 RS); the 2025/2026 tests are the closest available.
- **No Swiss-specific (SECO/BAFU) recall database query** was possible this run; Swiss recalls generally mirror the EU/manufacturer campaigns already captured (KTM's "valid for all markets" recall explicitly covers CH).
- **Ducati Multistrada V4 — per-variant weight** (V4 vs V4 S vs V4 Rally vs V4 GT) not pinned to a single read official CH sheet; figures ~223–235 kg are from reviews/DBs.
- **KTM 1390 Super Duke GT (the MY2027 successor)** — not yet on sale; no CH price/spec/tests yet.
